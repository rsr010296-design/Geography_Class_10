01 · TYPES OF FARMING

Types of Farming in India

🌾 Agriculture is a primary activity that provides food for consumption and raw material for industries. Two-thirds of India's population is engaged in agricultural activities. Farming methods have changed significantly depending on physical environment, technological know-how and socio-cultural practices — ranging from subsistence to commercial type.

🔥

Primitive Subsistence Farming

tap to explore ▼

PRIMITIVE SUBSISTENCE

  • Slash and Burn agriculture — clear, burn, cultivate, shift
  • Small patches of land; primitive tools: hoe, dao, digging sticks
  • Depends on monsoon and natural soil fertility
  • No fertilisers; low land productivity
  • Farmer shifts when soil fertility decreases
  • Known as Jhumming in NE India
💪

Intensive Subsistence Farming

tap to explore ▼

INTENSIVE SUBSISTENCE

  • Practised in areas of high population pressure on land
  • Labour-intensive; high doses of biochemical inputs and irrigation
  • Obtains higher production from limited land
  • 'Right of inheritance' divides landholdings making them uneconomical
  • Farmers continue for lack of alternative livelihood
💰

Commercial Farming

tap to explore ▼

COMMERCIAL FARMING

  • Higher doses of modern inputs: HYV seeds, chemical fertilisers, insecticides, pesticides
  • Aim: higher productivity for market sale
  • Same crop may be commercial in one region, subsistence in another
  • Example: Rice = commercial in Punjab/Haryana, subsistence in Odisha
  • Degree of commercialisation varies by region
🍵

Plantation Farming

tap to explore ▼

PLANTATION FARMING

  • Type of commercial farming — single crop on large area
  • Interface of agriculture and industry
  • Capital intensive; migrant labourers used
  • All produce used as raw material for industries
  • Needs well-developed transport/communication network
  • Important crops: tea, coffee, rubber, sugarcane, banana
  • Tea (Assam, North Bengal), Coffee (Karnataka)

02 · CROPPING SEASONS

India's Three Cropping Seasons

❄️ Rabi Season

S
Sown: October to December (winter)

Harvested April to June (summer)

C
Key Crops:

Wheat, barley, peas, gram, mustard

R
Important Regions:

Punjab, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, J&K, Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh

🌬️ Winter precipitation from western temperate cyclones helps success of rabi crops. Green Revolution in Punjab, Haryana, western UP and Rajasthan also boosted rabi production.

🌧️ Kharif Season

S
Sown: With onset of monsoon

Harvested September–October

C
Key Crops:

Paddy, maize, jowar, bajra, tur (arhar), moong, urad, cotton, jute, groundnut, soyabean

🌾 Important rice-growing regions: Assam, West Bengal, coastal Odisha, AP, Telangana, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Maharashtra (Konkan coast), UP, Bihar. Three paddy crops in Assam, WB, Odisha: Aus, Aman, Boro.

☀️ Zaid Season

S
Short summer season between rabi and kharif

Crops: watermelon, muskmelon, cucumber, vegetables, fodder crops

📌 Sugarcane takes almost a year to grow — does not fit neatly into one season.

04 · TECHNOLOGICAL & INSTITUTIONAL REFORMS

Agricultural Reforms & Policies

⚠️ Sustained use of land without compatible techno-institutional changes hindered agricultural development. Most farmers still depend on monsoon and natural fertility despite irrigation development. Over 60% of India's population depends on agriculture for livelihood.

🏛️ Institutional Reforms (Post-Independence)

1
Land Reform

Main focus of the First Five Year Plan. Collectivisation, consolidation of holdings, cooperation, abolition of zamindari.

2
Consolidation of Holdings

Right of inheritance led to fragmentation of land — consolidation became necessary to make holdings economically viable.

3
Bhoodan–Gramdan Movement

Vinoba Bhave's Bloodless Revolution. Began at Pochampally, Telangana. Shri Ram Chandra Reddy donated 80 acres for 80 landless villagers. Bhoodan (land gift) → Gramdan (village gift).

4
Grameen Banks & Cooperative Societies

Established to provide loans to farmers at lower rates of interest.

🔬 Technological Reforms

1
Green Revolution (1960s–70s)

Package technology — HYV seeds, chemical fertilisers, insecticides, irrigation. Led to concentration of development in selected areas.

2
White Revolution — Operation Flood

Strategy to improve dairy and milk production, improving rural livelihoods.

3
1980s–90s: Comprehensive Land Development

Both institutional and technical reforms. Crop insurance against drought, flood, cyclone, fire, disease introduced.

4
Farmers' Support Schemes

Kisan Credit Card (KCC), Personal Accident Insurance Scheme (PAIS), weather bulletins, agricultural programmes on radio/TV, Minimum Support Price (MSP).

📝 Short Answer Questions (30 words) NCERT

  1. Name one important beverage crop and specify the geographical conditions required for its growth.
    Tea is an important beverage crop. Geographical conditions: (i) Tropical and sub-tropical climate; (ii) Deep, fertile, well-drained soil rich in humus and organic matter; (iii) Warm and moist frost-free climate throughout the year; (iv) Frequent and evenly distributed rainfall (ensuring continuous growth of tender leaves); (v) Abundant, cheap and skilled labour. Tea is processed within the tea garden to restore freshness. Major states: Assam, West Bengal (Darjeeling, Jalpaiguri), Tamil Nadu, Kerala.
  2. Name one staple crop of India and the regions where it is produced.
    Rice is the staple food crop of a majority of people in India. It is the second largest producer of rice in the world after China. Regions: plains of north and north-eastern India (Assam, West Bengal, Bihar, UP), coastal areas and deltaic regions (Odisha, AP, Telangana, TN, Kerala, Maharashtra/Konkan coast). Canal irrigation and tubewells have extended rice cultivation to Punjab, Haryana, western UP and parts of Rajasthan.
  3. Enlist the various institutional reform programmes introduced by the government in the interest of farmers.
    Institutional reforms introduced by the government: (i) Land reforms — consolidation of holdings, abolition of zamindari, collectivisation; (ii) Crop insurance against drought, flood, cyclone, fire and disease; (iii) Grameen banks and cooperative societies for loans at lower interest rates; (iv) Kisan Credit Card (KCC) for easy credit access; (v) Personal Accident Insurance Scheme (PAIS); (vi) Special weather bulletins and agricultural programmes on radio and TV; (vii) Minimum Support Price (MSP) to protect farmers from exploitation by speculators and middlemen.

📖 Long Answer Questions (120 words) NCERT

  1. Suggest the initiatives taken by the government to ensure the increase in agricultural production.
    The government has taken the following initiatives to increase agricultural production: (i) Land Reforms — consolidation of holdings, abolition of zamindari, cooperative farming — were the main focus of the First Five Year Plan; (ii) Green Revolution (1960s–70s) — use of HYV seeds, chemical fertilisers, insecticides and improved irrigation; (iii) White Revolution (Operation Flood) — to improve dairy production; (iv) Comprehensive Land Development Programme (1980s–90s) — covering both institutional and technical reforms; (v) Crop Insurance against natural disasters; (vi) Grameen Banks and Cooperatives for easy credit; (vii) Kisan Credit Card (KCC) and Personal Accident Insurance Scheme (PAIS); (viii) Minimum Support Price (MSP) to protect farmers from middlemen; (ix) Weather bulletins and agricultural programmes on radio/TV; (x) Bhoodan–Gramdan Movement by Vinoba Bhave — voluntary land redistribution to the landless poor.
  2. Describe the geographical conditions required for the growth of rice.
    Rice is the staple food crop of India and the country is the second largest producer after China. Geographical conditions required: (i) Temperature: High temperature above 25°C throughout the growing season; (ii) Rainfall: High humidity with annual rainfall above 100 cm. In areas of less rainfall, irrigation (canal/tubewell) is essential; (iii) Soil: Grows well in plains, coastal areas and deltaic regions with fertile alluvial soils; (iv) Season: It is a Kharif crop, grown with the onset of monsoon; (v) Labour: Requires abundant and cheap labour for transplantation and harvesting; (vi) Regions: Rice is grown in the plains of north and north-eastern India, coastal and deltaic regions of Odisha, AP, TN, Kerala, WB and Maharashtra. Irrigation has extended cultivation to Punjab, Haryana, western UP and parts of Rajasthan.
