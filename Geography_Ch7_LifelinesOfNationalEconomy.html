01 · TRANSPORT — ROADWAYS

Roadways

🛣️ India has the second largest road network in the world, aggregating to about 62.16 lakh km (2020-21). Roadways preceded railways in India and still have an edge due to ease of construction, door-to-door service and suitability for short distances.

📋 Classification of Roads in India

TypeDescription
Golden Quadrilateral Super Highways6-lane highways linking Delhi–Kolkata–Chennai–Mumbai–Delhi. North-South corridor: Srinagar to Kanniyakumari. East-West corridor: Silchar (Assam) to Porbandar (Gujarat). Implemented by NHAI.
National HighwaysLink extreme parts of the country — primary road systems running N-S and E-W.
State HighwaysLink state capital with district headquarters.
District RoadsConnect district headquarters with other district places.
Other RoadsRural roads linking villages with towns. Special boost under Pradhan Mantri Grameen Sadak Yojana.
Border RoadsBuilt by Border Roads Organisation (est. 1960) for strategic importance in northern and north-eastern border areas. Also built Atal Tunnel (9.02 km — world's longest highway tunnel).

🏔️ Atal Tunnel (9.02 km) — World's longest highway tunnel, built by Border Roads Organisation. Connects Manali to Lahul-Spiti valley throughout the year. Located in Pir Panjal range at 3000m MSL. Earlier the valley was cut off for ~6 months due to heavy snowfall.

✅ Advantages of Roadways over Railways:
(a) Lower construction cost
(b) Can traverse dissected and undulating topography
(c) Can negotiate higher gradients — can cross Himalayas
(d) Economical for fewer persons and smaller goods over short distances
(e) Provides door-to-door service — lower loading/unloading cost
(f) Acts as feeder to railways, airports and seaports

🏗️ Types by Material: Metalled roads (cement, concrete or bitumen of coal) — all-weather roads. Unmetalled roads — go out of use in rainy season.

02 · TRANSPORT — RAILWAYS

Railways

🚂 Railways are the principal mode of transportation for freight and passengers in India. Indian Railways has been a great integrating force for more than 150 years. It is the largest public sector undertaking in the country. First train: Mumbai to Thane in 1853 (34 km). Now reorganised into 17 zones.

🗺️ Distribution Pattern of Railways

✓
Northern Plains — Most Favourable

Vast level land, high population density, rich agricultural resources. Some obstacles due to rivers needing bridges.

✗
Peninsular Region — Challenging

Hilly terrains — railway tracts laid through low hills, gaps or tunnels. Konkan Railway developed along west coast.

✗
Himalayan Region — Unfavourable

High relief, sparse population, lack of economic opportunities.

✗
Other Difficult Areas

Sandy plains of western Rajasthan, swamps of Gujarat, forested tracts of MP, Chhattisgarh, Odisha and Jharkhand.

03 · PIPELINES & WATERWAYS

Pipelines & Waterways

🔧 Pipelines

Pipeline transport is a new arrival on India's transportation map. Used for transporting crude oil, petroleum products and natural gas from fields to refineries, fertilizer factories and thermal power plants. Solids can also be transported when converted into slurry. Initial cost high but running costs minimal. Rules out trans-shipment losses or delays.

1
Upper Assam → Kanpur

Via Guwahati, Barauni and Prayagraj. Branches: Barauni→Haldia, Rajbandh→Maurigram, Guwahati→Siliguri.

2
Salaya (Gujarat) → Jalandhar (Punjab)

Via Viramgam, Mathura, Delhi and Sonipat. Branches to Koyali (Vadodara), Chakshu and other places.

3
HVJ Pipeline (Gas)

First 1,700 km long Hazira–Vijaipur–Jagdishpur cross-country gas pipeline linking Mumbai High and Bassein fields with western and northern India. Expanded to 18,500 km.

🌊 Waterways

Waterways are the cheapest means of transport, most suitable for carrying heavy and bulky goods. Fuel-efficient and environment friendly. India has 14,500 km of inland navigation waterways. 111 National Waterways declared under National Waterways Act, 2016 (including 5 NWs declared earlier). 95% of India's trade volume (68% in value) moved by sea.

✈️ Airways

Air travel is the fastest, most comfortable and prestigious mode of transport. It can cover very difficult terrains — high mountains, dreary deserts, dense forests and long oceanic stretches. Especially important for north-eastern states (big rivers, dissected relief, dense forests, frequent floods).

✈
Pawanhans Helicopters Ltd.

Provides helicopter services to ONGC in off-shore operations and inaccessible areas — NE states, J&K, Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand.

🛫 UDAN (Ude Desh ka Aam Nagrik) — Regional Connectivity Scheme (RCS) by Ministry of Civil Aviation. First-of-its-kind scheme globally to jump-start the regional aviation market by making flying affordable for the common citizen. Encourages airlines to operate on regional and remote routes through enabling policies and incentives.

05 · COMMUNICATION

Communication

📮

Postal Network

Indian postal network is the largest in the world. Handles parcels and personal written communications. Cards and envelopes = first-class mail (airlifted). Book packets, newspapers, periodicals = second-class mail (surface mail). Six mail channels for large towns: Rajdhani, Metro, Green, Business, Bulk Mail and Periodical Channel.

📻

Mass Communication

All India Radio (Akashwani) — broadcasts in national, regional and local languages. Doordarshan — national TV channel, one of the largest terrestrial networks in the world. India = largest producer of feature films in the world. Newspapers published in about 100 languages and dialects. Most newspapers in Hindi, followed by English and Urdu.

📱

Telecom & Digital India

India has one of the largest telecom networks in Asia. More than two-thirds of villages covered with STD telephone facility. Uniform STD rates across India. Digital India Programme: IT (Indian Talent) + IT (Information Technology) = IT (India Tomorrow). Focuses on technology-enabled transformative change.

06 · INTERNATIONAL TRADE & TOURISM

International Trade & Tourism

📊 Trade — Exchange of goods among people, states and countries. International trade = trade between two countries. Advancement of international trade = index of economic prosperity = economic barometer for a country. No country can survive without international trade as resources are space bound.

E
Exports from India

Gems and jewellery, chemicals and related products, agriculture and allied products, IT/software services. India has emerged as a software giant earning large foreign exchange.

I
Imports to India

Petroleum crude and products, gems and jewellery, chemicals, base metals, electronic items, machinery, agriculture and allied products.

B
Balance of Trade

Favourable = Exports > Imports. Unfavourable = Imports > Exports. India has trade relations with all major trading blocks and all geographical regions.

🏛️ Tourism as a Trade

Tourism in India has grown remarkably over the past two decades. 95% of country's trade volume (68% in value) is moved by sea.

S
Government Tourism Schemes

Swadesh Darshan 2.0, Vibrant Village Programme, PRASHAD (Pilgrimage Rejuvenation and Spiritual Heritage Augmentation Drive), Paryatan Mitra

T
Types of Tourism

Heritage tourism, eco tourism, adventure tourism, cultural tourism, medical tourism and business tourism

B
Benefits of Tourism

Promotes national integration, supports local handicrafts and cultural pursuits, develops international understanding about India's culture and heritage, generates employment

📝 Short Answer Questions NCERT

  1. State any three merits of roadways.
    Three merits of roadways: (i) Lower construction cost — construction cost of roads is much lower than that of railway lines; (ii) Versatile terrain coverage — roads can traverse more dissected and undulating topography, negotiate higher gradients, and can cross mountains like the Himalayas; (iii) Door-to-door service — road transport provides door-to-door service, reducing loading and unloading costs, and serves as a feeder to other modes of transport like railways, airports and seaports.
  2. Where and why is rail transport the most convenient means of transportation?
    Rail transport is most convenient in the Northern Plains of India — comprising states like Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Punjab and Haryana. The reasons are: (i) Vast level land — flat terrain makes it easy and inexpensive to lay railway lines; (ii) High population density — ensures large volume of traffic (passengers and freight); (iii) Rich agricultural resources — generates substantial goods traffic for railways. These favourable physical and economic conditions have led to the densest railway network in the country being concentrated in the northern plains.
  3. What is the significance of the border roads?
    Border Roads Organisation (est. 1960) constructs and maintains roads in the bordering areas of the country. Their significance: (i) Strategic importance — these roads are of strategic importance in the northern and north-eastern border areas for defence purposes; (ii) Improved accessibility — they improve accessibility in areas of difficult terrain like the Himalayas and north-eastern hills; (iii) Economic development — they have helped in the economic development of border areas by connecting them with the rest of the country; (iv) Atal Tunnel — BRO built the world's longest highway tunnel (9.02 km) connecting Manali to Lahul-Spiti valley throughout the year.
  4. What is meant by trade? What is the difference between international and local trade?
    Trade refers to the exchange of goods among people, states and countries. The market is the place where such exchanges take place.

    International Trade: Trade between two countries. It may take place through sea, air or land routes. Advancement of international trade is an index of economic prosperity and is considered the economic barometer for a country. India has trade relations with all major trading blocks and all geographical regions of the world.

    Local Trade: Trade carried within cities, towns and villages. State level trade is carried between two or more states within a country.

📖 Long Answer Questions NCERT

  1. Why are the means of transportation and communication called the lifelines of a nation and its economy?
    Means of transportation and communication are called the lifelines of a nation because: (i) Movement of goods and services — goods and services do not move on their own from supply locations to demand locations; transport makes this movement possible, which is essential for economic activity; (ii) Development pace — the pace of development of a country depends upon the production of goods and services as well as their movement over space; efficient transport is therefore a prerequisite for fast development; (iii) National integration — railways, airways and waterways have been integrating forces for over 150 years, binding the economic life of the country; (iv) Trade facilitation — without efficient transport, international trade (the economic barometer of a country) cannot function; 95% of India's trade is moved by sea; (v) Communication support — transport and communication are complementary; today's global trade requires both; (vi) Social benefits — railways, airways, newspapers, radio, television and internet have been contributing to India's socio-economic progress; trades from local to international levels have added vitality to the economy.
