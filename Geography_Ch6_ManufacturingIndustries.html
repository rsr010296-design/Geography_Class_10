01 · IMPORTANCE & CLASSIFICATION

Importance & Classification of Industries

🏭 Manufacturing — Production of goods in large quantities after processing from raw materials to more valuable products. The economic strength of a country is measured by the development of manufacturing industries.

🌾

Modernises Agriculture

Industries help modernise agriculture (backbone of economy) and reduce heavy dependence on agricultural income by providing jobs in secondary and tertiary sectors.

👷

Eradicates Unemployment

Industrial development is a precondition for eradication of unemployment and poverty. Industries in tribal and backward areas bring down regional disparities.

💰

Foreign Exchange

Export of manufactured goods expands trade and commerce and brings in much needed foreign exchange.

🌍

National Prosperity

Countries that transform raw materials into a wide variety of finished goods of higher value are prosperous. India's prosperity lies in increasing and diversifying manufacturing industries.

📊 Classification of Industries NCERT

1
By Raw Material

Agro-based: Cotton, woollen, jute, silk textile, rubber, sugar, tea, coffee, edible oil
Mineral-based: Iron & steel, cement, aluminium, machine tools, petrochemicals

2
By Main Role

Basic/Key industries: Supply products as raw materials to manufacture other goods — iron & steel, copper smelting, aluminium smelting
Consumer industries: Goods for direct consumer use — sugar, toothpaste, paper, fans

3
By Capital Investment

Small scale industry: Maximum investment allowed = ₹1 crore on unit assets

4
By Ownership

Public sector: BHEL, SAIL — owned & operated by government
Private sector: TISCO, Bajaj Auto, Dabur
Joint sector: Oil India Ltd. (OIL) — public + private
Cooperative sector: Sugar industry Maharashtra, Coir industry Kerala

5
By Bulk and Weight

Heavy industries: Iron and steel — heavy raw materials and finished goods, high transportation cost
Light industries: Electrical goods — light raw materials and products

COTTON TEXTILES

  • First successful textile mill — Mumbai, 1854
  • Concentrated in cotton growing belt of Maharashtra & Gujarat
  • Spinning centralised in Maharashtra, Gujarat, Tamil Nadu
  • Weaving highly decentralised (handloom, powerloom, mills)
  • Textile industry = self-reliant & complete in value chain (raw material to highest value added products)
  • Only industry with complete value chain in India
  • Links with agriculture: farmers, cotton boll pluckers, ginning, spinning, weaving workers
🌿

Jute Textiles

tap to explore ▼

JUTE TEXTILES

  • India = largest producer of raw jute and jute goods
  • 2nd largest exporter after Bangladesh
  • First jute mill: Rishra, near Kolkata (1855)
  • After 1947 Partition: mills in India, ¾ of jute producing area went to Bangladesh
  • Most mills along Hugli river, West Bengal (narrow belt)
  • Factors: proximity to raw jute areas, water transport, cheap labour (WB, Bihar, Odisha, UP), Kolkata banking/port
  • Used for: gunny bags, mats, ropes, yarn, carpets, artefacts
🍬

Sugar Industry

tap to explore ▼

SUGAR INDUSTRY

  • India = 2nd largest producer of sugar in the world
  • India = 1st place in production of gur and khandsari
  • Raw material bulky — sucrose reduces in haulage, so mills located near sugarcane areas
  • 60% mills in Uttar Pradesh and Bihar
  • Shifting trend to southern/western states (Maharashtra) — higher sucrose content, cooler climate = longer crushing season
  • Seasonal industry → ideally suited to cooperative sector
  • Also: Maharashtra, Karnataka, TN, AP, Gujarat, Punjab, Haryana, MP

Iron & Steel

tap to explore ▼

IRON & STEEL

  • The basic industry — all other industries depend on it for machinery
  • Production & consumption of steel = index of development
  • Raw material ratio: Iron ore : Coking coal : Limestone = 4:2:1
  • Heavy industry — all raw materials and finished goods are heavy and bulky
  • Chhotanagpur plateau has maximum concentration (low cost iron ore, high grade raw materials, cheap labour)
  • Plants: Jamshedpur, Bokaro, Burnpur, Durgapur, Rourkela, Bhilai, Bhadravati, Salem, Vishakhapatnam, Vijaynagar
✈️

Aluminium Smelting

tap to explore ▼

ALUMINIUM SMELTING

  • 2nd most important metallurgical industry in India
  • Light, resistant to corrosion, good conductor of heat, malleable
  • Used for aircraft, utensils, wires
  • Substitute for steel, copper, zinc and lead
  • Raw material: bauxite (4–6 tonnes → 2 tonnes alumina → 1 tonne aluminium)
  • Key factor: regular electricity supply + assured raw material
  • Plants: Odisha, West Bengal, Kerala, UP, Chhattisgarh, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu

Chemical Industry

CHEMICAL INDUSTRY

  • Fast growing and diversifying
  • Inorganic: sulphuric acid, nitric acid, alkalies, soda ash, caustic soda — widely spread
  • Organic: petrochemicals — used for synthetic fibres, rubber, plastics, dye-stuffs, drugs — located near oil refineries
  • Chemical industry is its own largest consumer
  • Fertiliser: Nitrogenous (urea), Phosphatic (DAP), Complex (NPK). Potash entirely imported
  • Half of fertiliser production: Gujarat, TN, UP, Punjab, Kerala

CEMENT INDUSTRY

05 · INDUSTRIAL POLLUTION & ENVIRONMENTAL DEGRADATION

Industrial Pollution & Control

⚠️ Industries are responsible for four types of pollution: (a) Air (b) Water (c) Land (d) Noise. The polluting industries also include thermal power plants. Every litre of waste water discharged by industry pollutes eight times the quantity of fresh water.

💨 Air Pollution

High proportion of undesirable gases — sulphur dioxide, carbon monoxide. Airborne particulate materials: dust, sprays, mist, smoke. Emitted by chemical/paper factories, brick kilns, refineries, smelting plants, burning fossil fuels. Toxic gas leaks can have long-term hazardous effects (Bhopal Gas Tragedy).

💧 Water Pollution

Caused by organic and inorganic industrial wastes discharged into rivers. Main culprits: paper, pulp, chemical, textile, dyeing, petroleum refineries, tanneries, electroplating — release dyes, detergents, acids, salts, heavy metals (lead, mercury), pesticides. Thermal pollution: hot water drained into rivers before cooling.

🌍 Land Pollution

Dumping of glass, harmful chemicals, industrial effluents, packaging, salts and garbage renders soil useless. Rain water carries pollutants to groundwater. Major solid wastes: fly ash, phospho-gypsum, iron and steel slags. Nuclear power plant wastes cause cancers, birth defects, miscarriages.

🔊 Noise Pollution

Results in irritation, anger, hearing impairment, increased heart rate and blood pressure. Caused by industrial machinery, factory equipment, generators, saws, pneumatic and electric drills, construction activities.

✅ Control of Environmental Degradation

1
Water Conservation

(i) Minimise water use — reuse and recycle in successive stages
(ii) Rainwater harvesting to meet water requirements
(iii) Treat hot water and effluents before releasing into rivers/ponds

2
Three Phases of Effluent Treatment

(a) Primary — mechanical: screening, grinding, flocculation, sedimentation
(b) Secondary — biological process
(c) Tertiary — biological, chemical and physical; involves recycling of wastewater

3
Air Pollution Control

Fit smoke stacks with electrostatic precipitators, fabric filters, scrubbers, inertial separators. Use oil/gas instead of coal in factories.

4
Noise Pollution Control

Machinery fitted with silencers. Redesign machinery for energy efficiency and noise reduction. Use noise-absorbing materials, earplugs, earphones.

🏆 NTPC — Major power providing corporation. ISO certified for EMS (Environment Management System) 14001. Proactive approach: green belts, ash pond management, ash water recycling, ecological monitoring.

📝 Short Answer Questions NCERT

  1. What is manufacturing?
    Manufacturing is the production of goods in large quantities after processing from raw materials to more valuable products. For example, paper is manufactured from wood, sugar from sugarcane, iron and steel from iron ore, and aluminium from bauxite. People employed in manufacturing are engaged in secondary activities and are part of the secondary sector of the economy. The economic strength of a country is measured by the development of its manufacturing industries.
  2. What are basic industries? Give an example.
    Basic or key industries are those which supply their products as raw materials to manufacture other goods. They form the foundation of industrial development. Examples: Iron and steel industry — the steel it produces is used as raw material by heavy, medium and light industries for their machinery. Other examples: copper smelting and aluminium smelting.

📖 Long Answer Questions NCERT

  1. How do industries pollute the environment?
    Industries pollute the environment in four ways: (i) Air Pollution: The presence of undesirable gases like sulphur dioxide and carbon monoxide, and airborne particulate materials (dust, sprays, mist, smoke) from chemical/paper factories, brick kilns, refineries and smelting plants pollute the air. Air pollution adversely affects human health, animals, plants and buildings. Toxic gas leaks can have long-term hazardous effects. (ii) Water Pollution: Industries like paper, pulp, chemical, textile, dyeing, petroleum refineries, tanneries and electroplating discharge dyes, detergents, acids, salts, heavy metals (lead and mercury), pesticides and synthetic chemicals into water bodies. Thermal pollution occurs when hot water from factories is drained into rivers before cooling. (iii) Land Pollution: Dumping of glass, harmful chemicals, industrial effluents, packaging, salts and garbage renders soil useless. Rain water percolates carrying pollutants to groundwater. Fly ash, phospho-gypsum and iron and steel slags are major solid wastes. (iv) Noise Pollution: Industrial activities, machinery, factory equipment, generators, saws, pneumatic and electric drills cause noise pollution leading to hearing impairment, increased heart rate and blood pressure.
  2. Discuss the steps to be taken to minimise environmental degradation by industry.
    Steps to minimise environmental degradation by industry: (i) Water Conservation: Minimise water use by reusing and recycling it in two or more successive stages; harvest rainwater to meet water requirements; treat hot water and effluents before releasing them into rivers and ponds. (ii) Effluent Treatment: Three phases — Primary (mechanical: screening, grinding, flocculation, sedimentation); Secondary (biological process); Tertiary (biological, chemical and physical processes involving recycling of wastewater). (iii) Air Pollution Control: Fit factory smoke stacks with electrostatic precipitators, fabric filters, scrubbers and inertial separators; use oil or gas instead of coal to reduce smoke. (iv) Noise Pollution Control: Fit generators with silencers; redesign machinery to reduce noise; use noise-absorbing materials and earplugs. (v) Groundwater Protection: Regulate overdrawing of groundwater by industries legally. (vi) NTPC's Approach: Optimum utilisation of equipment; minimise waste generation by maximising ash utilisation; provide green belts; reduce pollution through ash pond management; ecological monitoring for all power stations.
